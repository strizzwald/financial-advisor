# Client Scenario Library

Anonymised archetypes drawn from common financial planning situations in South Africa.
Use these as raw material for relatable, story-driven content. Never use real names.

---

## Scenario 1 — The Breadwinner with No Cover
**Age:** 38 | **Situation:** Married, 2 kids (6 and 9), bond of R1.2M outstanding
**Income:** R32,000/month (sole earner)
**Protection:** None — "I keep meaning to sort it out"
**Monthly spending:**
- Bond: R9,800
- Car: R4,200
- School fees: R3,600
- Groceries: R4,500
- Subscriptions/entertainment: R2,100
- Retirement: R0
- Life cover: R0

**The gap:** If he dies or becomes disabled tomorrow, his family loses the house within 3 months.
**Relevant products:** Life Cover, Capital Disability, Income Protector
**Content hook:** "He earns R32k a month and still has R0 in protection. Here's what that actually means for his family."

## Scenario 2 — The Young Professional Who Thinks She's Fine
**Age:** 26 | **Situation:** Single, renting, no dependants
**Income:** R18,500/month (first corporate job, 2 years in)
**Protection:** On parents' medical aid (expires at 27)
**Monthly spending:**
- Rent: R6,200
- Car: R3,100
- Takeaways / eating out: R2,800
- Streaming + gym + apps: R1,650 (often forgotten)
- Savings: R500 in a cheque account
- Retirement: R0
- Life/disability cover: R0

**The gap:** One bad accident and she has no income, no savings buffer, no disability cover.
**Relevant products:** Income Protector, Capital Disability, Medical Premium Protector
**Content hook:** "She earns R18k and thinks she's too young to worry about cover. Then her back gave out."

## Scenario 4 — The Family Man Who Can't Afford a Funeral
**Age:** 52 | **Situation:** Large extended family, parents still alive, 3 adult children
**Income:** R21,000/month
**Monthly spending:**
- Bond: R5,600
- Supporting parents: R2,500
- Car: R2,800
- Groceries (household of 5): R5,200
- Savings: R0
- Funeral cover: R0 (relies on stokvel "when the time comes")

**The gap:** A single funeral in the family costs R15,000–R40,000. The stokvel rarely covers it.
**Relevant products:** Funeral Benefit, Life Cover
**Content hook:** "The average funeral in SA costs R25,000. Most families don't have it — and find out the hard way."

## Scenario 7 — The Couple Who Ignored Disability Risk
**Age:** 37 | **Situation:** Car accident, 6 weeks in hospital, permanent partial disability
**Income before:** R29,000/month
**Income now:** R0 (employer paid 3 months, now exhausted)
**Cover in place:** Medical aid only
**Monthly gap:** R29,000 — still has bond, car, school fees, living costs

**Content hook:** "He thought disability only happened to other people. Then a taxi ran a red light."

## Scenario 8 — The Retiree Who Outlived Their Savings
**Age:** 68 | **Situation:** Retired at 60, expected to live to ~75, now 68 with money running out
**Pension:** Small government pension (R4,200/month)
**Savings at retirement:** R380,000 (now R90,000)
**Monthly needs:** R12,000
**Gap:** R7,800/month — children supplementing income

**The gap:** No life annuity or structured drawdown plan was in place at retirement.
**Relevant products:** Life Annuity with Capital Protection, Living Lifestyle Protector
**Content hook:** "She saved her whole life. At 68, she's asking her kids for money every month. Here's what went wrong."

# Sports & Fitness Audience Scenarios

These target active individuals — gym-goers, amateur athletes, personal trainers, coaches, and semi-professional sportspeople — who rely on their physical ability to earn a living or maintain their lifestyle. Their bodies are their biggest asset, yet they're often the least protected.

---

## Scenario 9 — The Personal Trainer Whose Income Stopped
**Age:** 29 | **Situation:** Full-time personal trainer, self-employed, 18 clients a week
**Income:** R22,000/month (entirely dependent on showing up and training)
**Protection:** Medical aid (basic plan), no disability or income cover
**Monthly commitments:**
- Rent: R6,500
- Car: R2,800
- Gym rental / equipment: R1,200
- Food / supplements: R2,500
- Savings: R0
- Income cover: R0

**What happened:** Torn ACL during a client session. Surgery + 4 months recovery. Zero income.
**The gap:** No clients trained = no money. Medical aid covered the surgery but not the 4 months of lost income.
**Relevant products:** Income Protector, Capital Disability
**Content hook:** "She trains 18 clients a week and earns R22k a month. A torn ACL took all of it away for 4 months. She had nothing to fall back on."

## Scenario 10 — The Amateur Rugby Player with a Day Job
**Age:** 31 | **Situation:** Plays club rugby on weekends, works as an electrician during the week
**Income:** R19,500/month — every cent earned physically (climbs, carries, works with hands)
**Protection:** UIF contributions only
**Monthly commitments:**
- Bond: R5,200
- Car: R3,100
- Kids (2): R4,800 in school fees + extras
- Disability cover: R0

**What happened:** Shoulder dislocation during a match. 8 weeks off work. Employer has no paid sick leave beyond 2 weeks.
**The gap:** Lost R12,000+ in income. Bond fell behind. Had to borrow from family.
**Relevant products:** Income Protector, Capital Disability, Impairment
**Content hook:** "He plays rugby for fun. But one tackle cost him R12,000 in lost wages and 8 weeks of stress he didn't see coming."

## Scenario 11 — The Gym Owner / Fitness Entrepreneur
**Age:** 36 | **Situation:** Owns a small CrossFit box, runs it herself, also coaches
**Income:** R35,000/month (business revenue — she IS the product)
**Staff:** 1 part-time coach
**Protection:** Nothing formal — "the business would just have to manage"
**Monthly costs:**
- Lease: R9,800
- Staff: R7,000
- Equipment finance: R2,100
- Personal drawings: R16,000
- Business income cover: R0

**What happened:** Stress fracture in her foot — 6 weeks off the floor. Members cancelled. Revenue dropped 40%.
**The gap:** When the owner can't coach, the business bleeds. No cover to bridge the gap.
**Relevant products:** Income Protector, Capital Disability, Financial Protector
**Content hook:** "She built her business from scratch. A stress fracture nearly closed it. There was nothing in place to protect the income while she healed."

## Scenario 12 — The Semi-Pro Athlete Transitioning Out of Sport
**Age:** 27 | **Situation:** Semi-professional cyclist, supplements income with coaching and brand deals
**Income:** R14,000/month (inconsistent — prize money, coaching, sponsorship)
**No formal employment benefits** — operates as an individual
**Savings:** R8,000 (3 weeks of expenses)

## Scenario 13 — The Marathon Runner Who Ignored the Risk
**Age:** 44 | **Situation:** Runs Comrades every year, gym 5x a week, desk job as an architect
**Income:** R52,000/month
**Fitness mindset:** "I'm healthy — I don't need much cover"
**Protection:** Life cover only (R500k), no disability
**Monthly commitments:**
- Bond: R14,200
- Two cars: R7,800
- Kids at private school: R9,600
- Retirement annuity: R3,000

**What happened:** Diagnosed with a degenerative knee condition at 44. No longer able to work on site. Desk-only — income reduced by 35%.
**The gap:** Fitness reduces health risk but does not eliminate disability risk. No income protector in place.
**Relevant products:** Income Protector, Capital Disability, Impairment
**Content hook:** "He runs Comrades. He goes to gym 5 times a week. He still ended up with a disability that cut his income by 35%."
