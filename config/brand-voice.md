# Brand Voice Guidelines

## Core Tone
- **Professional yet approachable** — Expert knowledge without jargon
- **Educational** — Teach, don't sell
- **Trustworthy** — Data-backed, transparent about limitations
- **Empathetic** — Acknowledge financial concerns and life situations

## Voice Characteristics
- **Clarity first** — Simple language wins
- **Human first** — Lead with a person's situation, not a product feature
- **Specific over vague** — "R1,800/month on subscriptions" beats "spending too much"
- **Conversational** — Like talking to a smart friend, not a textbook
- **Curious** — Ask questions, encourage thinking

## Content Structure (Story-First Formula)
1. **Open with a person** — age, situation, relatable detail (not the product)
2. **Reveal the gap** — what they're missing or what could go wrong
3. **Land the insight** — one clear takeaway
4. **Close with a question or CTA** — invite the audience in

### Example (correct)
> "A 34-year-old with two kids and a R1.2M bond has R0 in life cover. If something happens to him tomorrow, his family has 3 months before the bank calls."

### Example (avoid)
> "Life cover is an important product that protects your family in the event of death."

## Platform-Specific Adjustments

### LinkedIn
- More formal, professional tone
- Focus on financial principles and strategy
- Include thought leadership angle
- Longer form (500-1000 chars)
- CTA: "What's your approach?"

### Instagram / TikTok
- Very casual, energetic
- Quick facts and "aha!" moments
- Use relatable hooks
- Short form (60-150 chars)
- Emojis: Use sparingly for emphasis, not overload
- CTA: "Save this" or "Tag someone who needs this"

## Content Angles to Use
- **client-story** — Anonymised snapshot: "A 38-year-old client came to me with..."
- **before-after** — Contrast the financial situation before and after taking action
- **savings-tip** — Standalone money habit, no product required
- **financial-health-check** — "5 signs your finances need attention" style
- **misconception** — Bust a common myth about money or cover
- **real-cost** — What the gap actually costs in rands and sense
- **life-stage** — Specific to a life event (new baby, new job, marriage, divorce)

## Topics to Prioritize
- Protection gaps (what people overlook)
- Common misconceptions
- Life stage transitions (marriage, kids, business start)
- Real cost of doing nothing

## Product Mention Rules
- **Never explain how a product works** — that's what the consultation call is for
- **Name the category, not the mechanics** — say "income protection" not "a policy that pays 75% of your pre-disability income after a 3-month waiting period"
- **Use plain English labels** — "cover that pays your bills if you can't work" beats any product name
- **Always end with a soft CTA toward a conversation** — "DM me" / "Book a free call" / "Let's chat"
- The content's job is to make someone feel seen and curious — not to explain the product

## Topics to Avoid
- Specific investment recommendations
- Guarantees or certainty statements
- Comparative product bashing
- Financial advice (position as educational only)
- Technical product mechanics or policy terms

## Language Rules
- ❌ "You should" → ✅ "Consider"
- ❌ "Definitely will happen" → ✅ "Often happens"
- ✅ Always: "everyone's situation is different"
- ✅ Encourage: speak to a professional

## Caption & Headline Language
Captions must feel human, not like ad copy. Keep the focus on the family or person — not the product or transaction.

**Avoid — sounds like a sales brochure or payday loan ad:**
- ❌ "Fast cash for funeral costs — sorted in advance"
- ❌ "Get covered today"
- ❌ "Protect your family with the right product"
- ❌ Anything with "fast cash", "sorted", "get covered", or "the right solution"

**Use instead — lead with the person, the moment, or the emotion:**
- ✅ "So your family doesn't have to figure out the money while they're still crying."
- ✅ "One less thing they have to carry."
- ✅ "Grief is heavy enough without the bill."
- ✅ "The money should already be there."
- ✅ "Sort this one before the moment arrives."

The test: would a caring friend say this, or does it sound like a banner ad?

## Sign-off Examples
- LinkedIn: "What's your biggest [topic] concern?"
- Instagram/TikTok: "Drop a 💬 if this resonates"
